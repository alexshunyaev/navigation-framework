\section{Reflection: What Makes the System Intelligent?}

The system's intelligence is not in any one algorithm but in the interaction of
all ingredients.

\paragraph{Search, optimisation, and representation together.} \textbf{Search} (A*) supplies
global competence --- completeness and optimality over the map, so the robot never traps
itself in a dead end. \textbf{Optimisation} (DWA/MPPI) supplies local competence ---
continuously choosing dynamically feasible motions that trade off progress, safety and
smoothness against the live obstacle geometry. \textbf{Representation} (the probabilistic
risk field and the inflated planning grid) lets the two cooperate: it turns a binary world
into a graded one and guarantees both layers agree on what is drivable. Remove any one and the whole degrades: no search gives dead ends, no optimisation
undrivable paths, no shared representation two layers that fight.

\paragraph{Reasoning about the future.} Intelligence here is not a reflex to the current
instant. MPPI optimises a whole control sequence over a short horizon, scoring where each
candidate \emph{leads} rather than how it looks now --- so the robot commits to compound
manoeuvres (``turn, then go straight'') and accepts a momentarily worse state to reach a
better one, which a greedy step cannot do.

\paragraph{Balancing competing objectives.} The robot never optimises a single quantity;
every motion is judged by a weighted trade-off of progress, clearance, speed, smoothness and
path fidelity. Behaving well means \emph{negotiating} these goals when they conflict ---
closer to judgement than to a fixed rule.

\paragraph{Adaptation and generality.} The system is reactive: it re-solves its cost
minimisation every control step, with MPPI's receding-horizon warm start carrying
information forward, so the robot answers the obstacles in front of it. Nothing is
hand-crafted for a specific map, so the same pipeline solves arbitrary random worlds ---
competence in the method, not a memorised solution.
